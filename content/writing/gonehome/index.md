The story in the game Gone Home is one that is best conveyed in the medium of video games. Any other format would not have the same effect on the audience. To quote Henry Jenkins in Game Design as Narrative Architecture, “Game Designers don’t simply tell stories, they create worlds and sculpt spaces.” This reading encapsulates how the immersive nature of games stimulates the player’s emotions by making them feel as if they are a part of the world in a way other mediums, such as writing or film, cannot achieve. &emsp;

Jenkins’ commentary about how amusement parks create an immersive environment for their visitors through existing tropes and familiar stories particularly stands out as an exemplary use of the setting to create a specific atmosphere and mood. Gone Home uses this exact technique when conveying how the main character, Katie, is feeling while she searches for her sister, Sam. The game uses tropes from the horror genre despite not having anything directly scary or threatening. This environment primes the player’s expectation and builds anticipation that something shocking is about to happen. &emsp;

Throughout the game, the player is alone in the large house, the lights flicker, and the only sounds are their footsteps, the creaking house, and the storm outside. Despite most of these elements having a diegetic explanation, for example the lights flickering due to faulty wiring, they set the scene for a classic horror story. The player is tense as a result of these familiar, haunting tropes, which leads to an increasing buildup of expectation and emotion. &emsp;

As the game progresses and the player learns more about the story through the narration, Sam’s disappearance remains a mystery. The player’s assumption about where she is worsens as the player learns more about her life. In the story, she was bullied, and despite her romantic relationship with Lonnie, Sam and Katie’s parents still denied her sexuality. Lonnie even plans to go into the military soon, leaving Sam behind. The story itself seems to hint that while Sam felt frustrated at these situations, her feelings did not dive deeper into a depression. The game tells the player otherwise. Through the worldbuilding and set, the player is led to expect a sad ending, especially with elements that are easily misinterpreted like the red hair dye in the bathtub. The eerie environment allows the player to feel the exact anxiety that Katie may be feeling in that moment – that Sam may have taken her own life after the inability to handle the rejection from her parents and the departure of Lonnie. &emsp;

In an instant, the fear and anxiety built through the setting and mood are eradicated with the final narration of Gone Home. While it is bittersweet that Katie may never see Sam again after running away with Lonnie, Sam is still alive and living her happy ending, unlike what the player may have predicted. Despite using horror tropes, Gone Home is not a horror story. Just as amusement parks allow for visitors to dive into a world, as Jenkins mentions in his writing when discussing “environmental storytelling”, Gone Home employs the same techniques of world building to set the mood for its players. It uses the genre to push the anxiety of the player and create a compelling story where the player can experience what the main character is feeling before finding out what truly happened.&emsp;
