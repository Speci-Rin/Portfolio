The story in the game Gone Home is one that is best conveyed in the medium of video games. Any other format would not have the same effect on the audience. To quote Henry Jenkins in Game Design as Narrative Architecture, “Game Designers don’t simply tell stories, they create worlds and sculpt spaces.” This reading goes over how the immersive nature of games stimulates the player’s emotions through making them feel as if they are a part of the world in a way other mediums, such as writing or film, cannot achieve. &emsp;

A specific part of the reading stood out to me when the author wrote about how amusement parks create an immersive environment for their visitors through re-existing tropes and familiar stories. Gone Home uses this exact technique when conveying how the main character, Kate, is feeling while she searches for her sister, Sam. The game uses tropes from the horror genre despite not having anything directly scary or threatening. This environment molds the player’s expectations that the story is waiting to show them something shocking. &emsp;

Throughout the game, you are alone in the large house, the lights flicker, and the only sounds are your own footsteps, the creaking house, and the storm outside. Despite most of these elements having a diegetic explanation, for example the lights flickering due to faulty wiring, they set the scene for a classic horror story. The player is tense as a result from these familiar, haunting tropes. &emsp;

As the game progresses and the player learns more about the story through the narration, Sam’s disappearance remains a mystery. The player’s expectation for where she is worsens as the player learns more about her life. In the story, she was bullied, and despite her romantic relationship with Lonnie, Sam and Kate’s parents still denied her sexuality. Lonnie even plans to go into the military soon, leaving Sam behind. The story itself seems to hint that while Sam felt frustrated at these situations, her feelings did not dive deeper into a depression. The game tells the player otherwise. Through the worldbuilding and set, the player builds up the expectation for a sad ending, especially with elements that are easily misinterpreted like the red hair dye in the bathtub. The eerie environment allows the player to feel the exact anxiety that Katie may be feeling in that moment – that Sam may have taken her own life after the inability to handle the rejection from her parents and the departure of Lonnie. &emsp;

In an instant, the fear and anxiety built through the setting and mood are eradicated with the final narration. While it is bittersweet that Kate may never see Sam again after running away with Lonnie, Sam is still alive and living her happy ending, unlike what the player may have predicted. Despite using horror tropes, Gone Home is not a horror story. Instead, it uses the genre to push the anxiety of the player and create a compelling story where the player can feel what the main character is feeling before finding out what truly happened.&emsp;
