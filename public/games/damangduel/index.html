Background
#

  Growing up, I had always heard stories from my lola about her childhood, growing up in the Philippines. One such story that had always stood out to me was her experience of spider fighting. She told me how she would collect spiders from trees, store them in matchboxes, then take them to school to battle the other children. consisted of a single stick with each spider placed on either end. The spider who was killed first lost and the other spider would win. There would also be small bets involving candies, spiders, and small amounts of money. My lola always bragged of winning every single fight she had entered.  

These stories led me to dreaming up ways I could experience this myself here in America. This led me down the path to creating a game that encapsulated this activity in a digital format. I started drawing storyboards and concept art from what this might look like, and thus began my journey towards creating Damang Duel: Spider Showdown. This game would be a futuristic spider fighting game that included mechanical spider robots fighting to win. Each player will also have a chance to bet on each round of the fight. This iteration was made during Pitt’s Digital Narrative and Interactive Design English Capstone.  

My Role
#

My role in this project was the Project Leader, Concept Artist and Gameplay Designer.  

As the Project Leader, I was in charge of ensuring this project executed my idea thoroughly and authentically. To do this, I discussed with my project partner what the game should look like at the beginning of each week and kept track of the different deadlines. 

I drew potential spider designs on paper and on Clip Studio Paint as the Concept Artist. I also drew mock screenshots and some story boards for the game’s aesthetic throughout the project. Lastly, I sent out a form for feedback on these designs and planned iterations moving forward. For the Gameplay Designer, I worked on what the game would look and feel like during the fighting section. I designed the different movesets of each spider design and implemented two of them into the game.  

The Game
#

Damang Duel: Spider Showdown is a multi-player 2D fighting game for pc where the players must win matches of fights against the other player to win the pot of money. The players must bet on and fight spiders so that they mimic the real sport of spider fighting from the Philippines. They must also master mechanics and overpower their friends so that they bond and feel excitement in the couch multiplayer setting. This game had a few key goals in order to succeed. First, the game must express the elements of spider fighting through a digital context. I wanted the main game play loop to be inspired by the original activity’s rules and traditions. The second goal was that it must be representative of Filipino culture while also being accessible to a wider audience. I wanted people from the Philippines to recognize the references throughout the game, but I also wanted those who are unfamiliar with the culture to come to understand some of these references. This problem was approached by choosing inspiration for each spider design to correlate with a common part of Filipino culture. An appendix was also created to inform the player of what each inspiration stems from. 

The Process
#

Research
#

The beginning of this whole process included research into the field. As I am Filipino American, I first decided to read anything pertaining to spider fighting specifically. I then moved into more general history and culture in the Philippines. I also looked into aesthetic and gameplay references such as Ghost in the Shell and Divekick. As the project progressed, this research served as a basis to inform our decisions moving forward. For instance, an article titled “Spider Wrestling and Gambling Culture in the Rural Philippines on JSTOR” both supported the information I had collected from my Lola and further informed us of the gambling culture surrounding spider fighting, referred to as spider wrestling in this particular paper. This led to our inclusion of betting mechanics and our decision to keep it fairly simple.

Design Shift
#

The initial direction of the game was to create a single-player experience that was in conversation with the history of colonialism in the Philippines. The game consisted of a more narrative direction, with the player climbing a vertical map, fighting different colonial powers as they try to reach the top. While thinking of specific narrative beats, we had realized that while we loved the concept, crafting the narrative around it was not clicking with us. This led us to planning out a four-section graph to jot down how we might imagine the game with different parameters. Our two axes were 1 player versus 2 multiplayer and colonialism focus vs spider fighting focus. In the photo, the spider fighting point was labeled “gambling” as this was when we introduced the idea of adding a betting system to nod towards how the sport is played in the Philippines. 

Brainstorm Page of Different Game Directions As a quick overview of our brainstorming from each section, I will review them in clockwise order with the photo as reference. Our single player, gambling mechanic game was leaning towards a gotcha game similar to Umamusume or Pokémon. While this seemed fun, it felt as if it went against our goal of expressing the elements of spider fighting as the main element was the fight, not the bet. The single player, colonization narrative matched the original concept, but it is also why we started this brainstorm. The multiplayer, colonization narrative was very similar to the original concept with an additional player. Some touchstones would have been Split Fiction or Bokura. This idea did not seem strong or different enough to pursue for this topic.  

Finally, the idea we saw through was the multiplayer, gambling mechanic game. We decided the players would each have a team of 3 spiders to fight in different rounds. Each spider would have design ties to Filipino culture. The focus on player interaction attracted us to this idea the most. 

This last idea of shifting to a multiplayer, fighting game that included gambling mechanics sparked our interest the most, so we decided to pivot in this direction.

Concept Art
#

The initial art direction labeled the aesthetic as “Cyber Punk,” as one of the original touch stones was Ghost in the Shell. While this aesthetic allowed for the more creative designs for the spider mechs, the aesthetic as a whole did not fully represent the new direction. A cyber punk world fits into a narrative design focused around colonization, but with the new design centering around interaction between players, the desolate vibe was no longer connected. The spider designs were also a mix of colonial influence and Filipino influence, which needed to shift to designs fully influenced by Filipino culture. 

Spanish-influenced Catholic Spider Designs 

Darker background concept art leaning towards the cyber punk aesthetic 

I played around with a lot of different ideas to accommodate this new direction. One touchstone I had found in this search was the Chobani Ad, “Eat Today, Feed Tomorrow,” that exhibited a solar-punk aesthetic. This aesthetic was a lot more hopeful than cyber punk, and it often showed bright scenes with technology intertwined with nature. This was closer to what we wanted, but it was missing the culture element. This is when we found the sci-fi Filipino comic, Maharlika. This showed a futuristic world where the Philippines has taken over the world. With a mix of both aesthetics, the concept art took a dive into a speculative future.

Prototyping
#

The current version of the game has the basis of the game without implemented art. This version shows what I worked on in the general game play loop with 2 spiders with different moves implemented. The main difference between them is that one spider is able to dash and double jump while the other is able to grapple the ceiling. There is also an appendix that explains the references on each spider’s design.  

Looking Ahead
#

In the end, this project was a culmination of everything I have studied up to this point. I was able to demonstrate my skills in leadership, programming, concept art, and writing. I learned that sometimes a design shift is all it takes to bring an idea to life, and collaboration is an extremely useful tool to do so. We were able to adapt each iteration with outside feedback to satisfy our original goals for the game. Moving forward, I hope to develop this game further into a final product. I would like to work with an artist and animator to make Damang Duel come to life. Other things that can be done include implementing more spider mechanics, creating a dynamic camera, and creating an online option. I would also like to expand on the moves that the current spiders are able to do.
